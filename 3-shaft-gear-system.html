Executive Summary & System Requirements

This report documents the design of a compact, highly reliable three-shaft gear train system that transmits 60 kW of input power from an electric motor operating at 2700 RPM (Shaft A) to an output shaft (Shaft C) operating at 399.94 RPM (targeting 400 ± 3 RPM).

The design targets 99% operational reliability for continuous operation over a 5-year service life. The team achieved an optimized layout minimizing total shaft centerline distance (AC = 405 mm) while ensuring all gears, shafts, and SKF ball bearings exceed strict bending, surface fatigue, and static yield limits.

Gear Kinematic & Stress Analysis

Two double-reduction spur gear pairs were designed with a standard 5 mm module and 20° pressure angle. Gears 1 and 3 were manufactured from heat-treated AISI 4140 steel, while driven Gears 2 and 4 were fabricated from quenched & tempered AISI 1045 steel.

SKF Bearing Selection & Maintenance Plan

SKF Deep Groove Ball Bearings were selected across all three shafts due to zero axial thrust load generation from spur gears and low frictional torque at high input speeds (2700 RPM).

Maintenance & Operational Strategy

  • Zero Bearing Replacement: Calculated bearing life exceeds the 5-year continuous service benchmark, requiring no mid-life replacement services.
  • Annual Oil Lubrication Cycle: Recommended annual gear oil drainage and refill; fitted with elastomeric felt seals to protect against particle ingress and oil leakage.
  • Standardized Components: Standardized SKF 6309 bearings on input and output shafts reduce assembly tooling complexity and inventory costs.
