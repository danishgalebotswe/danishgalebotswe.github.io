Introduction & Reverse Engineering CAD Strategy

This project focused on designing and manufacturing a custom phone case for the Google Pixel 7 using advanced multi-material additive manufacturing.

To achieve a precise, snug fit around the Pixel 7's curved body and rear camera bar, precision digital calipers were used to reverse-engineer all critical dimensions. SolidWorks surface modeling tools—including Sweep Surface, Surface Fill, Knit Surface, and Thicken—were utilized to construct the negative cavity geometry.

Three-Stage Iterative Manufacturing Review

Iteration Stage AM Process & Material Primary Focus / Evaluation Key Refinement Outcome
Model 1 (Prototype 1) FDM (Prusa MK4) / Flexible TPU Size, feature cutouts & dimensional fit test Cutouts aligned well, but 1mm TPU was too flexible for snap retention. Shifted to SLA resins.
Model 2 (Prototype 2) SLA (Formlabs Form 4) / Tough 1500 Ergonomics, tactile button feel & user study Tested with 5 users. Feedback showed power button required too much force, and axial fan snap-on was too low.
Model 3 (Final Version) SLA / Tough 1500 Body + Tough 2000 Fan Final assembly, surface finish & accessory optimization Relocated axial fan higher for comfortable finger clearance; combined Tough 1500 body with stiffer Tough 2000 fan blades.

Structured User Ergonomic Testing (5 Participants)

A structured questionnaire was conducted with 5 external evaluators on Model 2 to evaluate tactile feel, ease of insertion, power button actuation, and fidget fan usability.

  • Power Button Force: 4 out of 5 users noted high button actuation effort. In Model 3, button flexure geometry was thinned to restore tactile travel click.
  • Axial Fan Position & Clearance: All users requested moving the snap-on fidget fan higher up the case and increasing finger clearance behind the blades.
  • Material Toughness: Transitioned fan blades to Formlabs Tough 2000 resin to eliminate trailing-edge flex while retaining Tough 1500 for shock absorption on the main case body.
